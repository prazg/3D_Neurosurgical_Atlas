Advanced 3D Surgical
Anatomy Atlas — Brain & Spine

An interactive 3D atlas of craniospinal anatomy and neuro-oncological pathology. Structures are clickable and labelled with anatomy notes, operative approaches and pitfalls, with captioned reference illustrations, cadaveric plates from the Rhoton Collection, lesion-localisation and functional-pathway walkthroughs, and a live 2D section view.

Open the atlasMobile version

Desktop loads ~9 MB · needs WebGL · phone-friendly mobile version available

Anatomy & Skull Base

Craniospinal specimen, 327-structure MRI brain, skull sutures & keypoints, 125 µm OpenEar temporal bone, TopCoW Circle of Willis variants, and PAM50 spinal cord tracts.

MNI Overlay & Caliper

Co-registered HCP-1065 tracts, VENAT 7T cerebral veins, CIT168/AAN/SUIT deep nuclei & cerebellar lobules, arterial territories, DBS targets and 3D mm caliper.

76 Guided Walkthroughs

34 cranial, skull-base, functional & spinal approaches, 23 cranial/spinal lesion syndromes, 17 pathways & safe-entry guides, and left + right Klingler dissections.

Pathology & 2D Section

Saccular aneurysms, 9 meningioma sites, GBM & WHO 2021 gliomas, longitudinal SRS brain metastases, paediatric tumours, vestibular schwannoma, Rhoton plates & live 2D section.

Educational use only. A teaching aid built from open, de-identified datasets. Not a medical device, not patient-specific, and not for diagnosis, operative planning or navigation. Craniotomy footprints and entry points are schematic approximations.

Built from open anatomical and imaging collections — BodyParts3D, the SPL atlases, HCPex, HCP-1065, JHU, Jülich, VENAT 7T veins, CIT168, Harvard AAN, SUIT, OpenEar temporal bone, PAM50 spinal cord, TopCoW, BraDiPho left & right fibre dissections, TCIA/BraTS/PROTEAS/RSNA pathology cohorts, Servier Medical Art illustrations and Rhoton Collection plates (courtesy of the Rhoton Collection, American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS)/Neurosurgical Research and Education Foundation (NREF)) — each under its own terms. Full attribution is in the atlas's Credits & licence panel and in the repository.
